Model description

Avinash HM builds large language model systems that survive contact with a budget.

He came to machine learning through electronics. Four years of Electronics and Communication Engineering at Sapthagiri College in Bangalore, finishing in 2023, is an education in constraint — in what fits on a board, what a power envelope permits, what you do when the hardware refuses to grow. That turns out to be the correct training data for the era of models too large to deploy.

An MBA in Data Analytics and Marketing at IFIM College followed, through 2025, adding the second half of the problem: not just whether a system works, but whether it is worth running. Most engineers pick one of these. The combination is the reason his work tends to be measured in percentages rather than adjectives.

The thesis arrived at Reliance Jio in early 2025. Fine-tuning transformer models for edge deployment, he cut inference latency by about 35% and cloud dependency by about 40%, then applied GPTQ and AWQ quantization for roughly 45% memory reduction while holding more than 95% of baseline accuracy. The finding generalised: the model does not have to be smaller, it has to be cheaper to be right. That work became a benchmark study across Qwen-3, LLaMA-3.2 and GPT-2, and then an IEEE conference paper at ICIICS-2026 on post-training quantization.

Since July 2025 he has been at AiGenthix Technologies, where the work moved from single models to systems that co-ordinate: five-plus production LLM deployments, RAG architectures that lifted retrieval relevance around 30% over keyword baselines, and multi-agent workflows built on A2A interaction patterns and the Model Context Protocol, cutting manual effort by about 40%. Along the way, a multi-agent education platform called Sahayak AI took second place nationally at Google Cloud and Hack2Skill's Agentic AI Day 2025.

The through-line is compression in every sense: of models, of cost, of the distance between a research result and something a company can run on Monday.

Limitations and out-of-scope use

In scope

  • Production LLM systems: RAG pipelines, agentic workflows, tool use and orchestration.
  • Inference economics — quantization, latency and memory work on constrained or edge hardware.
  • Evaluation and benchmarking of model versions before release.
  • Containerised deployment on Docker and Kubernetes with CI/CD.

Out of scope

  • One year of production experience. Not a decade-deep distributed systems architect, and does not claim to be.
  • Pre-training foundation models from scratch; the work is post-training, serving and systems.
  • Not a substitute for a team. Strongest where an organisation already has a problem worth measuring.
